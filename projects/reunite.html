02 — The problem

Two systems that never talk to each other

NTU’s lost & found ran on two things that didn’t work well together — and both failed the same way: the person who lost something and the person who found it never met.

Telegram channel

Hundreds of unstructured messages. No search, photos buried where you don’t need them, and anything more than a week old is effectively gone.

Logbook @ One-Stop

A physical book (and a Google Sheet) at One-Stop@SAC. You have to walk there to check — and walk there again to report.

Someone hands in a wallet, someone else spends a week looking for it, and neither ever knows about the other. ReUnite closes that gap.

04 — What it does

Seven flows, one goal

Report a found item

The whole flow is a photo and two locations.

  • Photograph the item and Gemini writes a detailed description — edit or regenerate it.
  • In-app camera capture with front/rear switching.
  • Live maps for both locations: search, drop a pin, paste coordinates, or use your location.
  • The drop-off location stays private — revealed only to whoever claims it.

Search for what you lost

Text search that forgives how people actually remember things.

  • Fuzzy matching across titles, descriptions and locations — typos still land.
  • Filter and sort by status, date found, or expiry; grid or list.
  • Every search is a URL — shareable, bookmarkable, back-button friendly.
  • Server-rendered results; the empty state hands you to the alert form, pre-filled.

Search with a photo

“I don’t know what to call it, but I know what it looks like.”

  • Upload or snap your lost item; AI describes it, then a custom engine ranks every found item.
  • A similarity score on every result, so you know if it’s worth the walk.
  • Your photo never leaves your device — only the generated description is sent.

Claiming an item

Real identities, and no two people showing up for the same wallet.

  • Google sign-in attaches a real identity to every claim.
  • The pickup location is emailed to the claimant and shown nowhere else.
  • Atomic claiming — two simultaneous taps can’t both win.
  • Every claim is recorded and reversible; dispute guidance (Campus Security, PDPA) is built in.

Match alerts

“Tell me when it turns up.”

  • Describe your item (or let AI describe a photo) and leave an email.
  • Every new report is compared against it — automatically, daily, for 30 days.
  • The top three matches are emailed with photos, scores, and a claim link.
  • A relevance floor and once-a-day cap keep it from ever being spammy; auto-deleted at 30 days.

Admin console

Everything needed to run it, properly locked down.

  • Live dashboard: totals, claimed/unclaimed, expiring soon, active alerts, claim rate, trends.
  • Correct a record, extend a listing, reverse a claim (returns it to the pool), or remove it.
  • Matching diagnostics: rank live data with the previous algorithm’s score alongside.
  • Server-verified sessions, role checks on every route and mutation, typed confirmation to delete.

Automated housekeeping

Runs itself, once a day.

  • Expired listings are removed — and so are their photos, so nothing lingers in storage.
  • Expired alerts and the email addresses attached to them are deleted.
  • Everyone waiting on an alert is re-checked against everything newly reported.
  • Idempotent: it compares absolute timestamps, so running twice — or missing a day — lands identically.

06 — Outcome & reflection

What happened, and what I’d do next

Outcome

ReUnite took the “Most Useful” award against 50+ teams, with interest in adopting it across NTU. I led a team of five, owned the AI matching pipeline, and kept scope tight enough to demo something that genuinely worked inside the hackathon window.

What I’d change

Visual similarity was the differentiator and also the most fragile part — real photos are badly lit and cluttered. Next time I’d add a confidence threshold with human-in-the-loop confirmation before notifying an owner, so a bad match never becomes a false alarm.

07 — What’s next

Where ReUnite goes from here

NTU adoption Seek official university backing so it runs as the campus lost & found.
Scale beyond campus Generalise the model to any venue — airports, transit, other universities.
Native mobile app iOS/Android builds so reporting is one tap from the lock screen.
Sharper matching Confidence-gated, human-in-the-loop image matching to kill false alarms.
