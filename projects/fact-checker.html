02 — The problem

Misinformation moves faster than fact-checkers

It spreads fastest where people already are — in group chats. By the time someone thinks to open a fact-checking site, the article has been forwarded twenty times.

The tools that exist

Dedicated fact-check sites are thorough — but they live off-platform. You have to stop, switch apps, and go looking. Almost nobody does that mid-scroll.

Where it actually spreads

In the chat, a forward is one tap and it carries a friend’s implicit trust. There’s no friction, no pause, and no prompt to ask whether it’s true.

The goal was never to declare something “fake” — it was to slow the reader down for five seconds before they hit forward.

04 — What it does

Four things, one habit change

Right in the chat

No app-switch, no friction.

  • Send a link or paste the text and get a credibility read back in the same thread.
  • Replies stay conversational, so checking feels like asking a friend.

A tuned Gemini pipeline

Prompt engineering as the accuracy lever.

  • Assesses tone, sourcing and claim structure rather than keyword matching.
  • Prompt strategy drove a 20% lift in detection over the naive baseline.

Fast enough to feel live

Speed keeps people using it.

  • API request tuning cut response time by 30%.
  • Fast replies mean the check happens before the forward, not after.

Reasoning, not a label

Show the why, not just the score.

  • Every verdict comes with plain-language reasoning behind it.
  • Testers said the reasoning made them re-read the article instead of trusting the number.

Beyond the bot: we also prototyped the flip side in Figma — instead of asking people to check a link, the concept nudges them to verify the moment they hit forward, turning a reflex into a two-second pause.

06 — Outcome & reflection

What happened, and what I’d do next

Outcome

Leading a team of five, I owned the prompt strategy behind the accuracy gains and shipped a working bot inside the festival window. Testers consistently said the reasoning mattered more than the score — it made them re-read the article instead of trusting the verdict.

What I’d change

A single model’s judgement is a starting point, not a verdict. I’d add source-reputation signals and cite the evidence behind each score, so users can judge the article for themselves rather than swapping one authority for another.

07 — What’s next

From one opinion to real evidence

Source-reputation signals Weight outlets by track record, not just the text of one article.
Cite the evidence Link the sources behind every score so readers can check for themselves.
More than one judge Cross-check multiple models to cut single-model bias out of the verdict.
Beyond Telegram The same in-context check on WhatsApp and the web.
